# About Pharlux

**Last updated: 2026-06-13** · Applies to: Pharlux v1.0.0

Pharlux is a single statically-linked Rust binary for self-hosted, OpenTelemetry-native observability. It ingests metrics and logs (traces in V1.1), stores them as per-signal Apache Parquet on local disk, and serves SQL queries, dashboards, and alerting from one process. It is built for small teams running 1–10 services on a single VPS — the people who need to observe their systems without standing up and operating a five-component stack to do it.

## Why Pharlux exists

Observability for a small team had quietly become a second system to run. Metrics in Prometheus, logs in Loki, dashboards in Grafana, alerts in Alertmanager, storage in an object store — five moving parts, five config files, five upgrade cycles, to watch three services. The alternative was a SaaS bill that scaled with every host and shipped your telemetry to someone else's cloud.

Pharlux is the third option: one binary you install with `sudo pharlux install`, one `pharlux.toml`, one systemd unit, on hardware you own. The design is a series of deliberate refusals — no Docker, no Kafka, no ClickHouse, no external database beyond embedded SQLite, no OpenSSL in the dependency tree. Each constraint forced a simpler system that a single operator can fully reason about.

## Who builds Pharlux

Pharlux is one of several developer tools built by [Veltara Works](https://veltaraworks.com/) — alongside email hosting, cloud infrastructure, and software license management. See [veltaraworks.com](https://veltaraworks.com/) for the full portfolio.

Pharlux is the observability layer Veltara Works runs on its own production stack, including ValidonX and Vectis Mail. The benchmarks and reliability claims on this site are first-party: measured on commodity hardware, reproducible from the source tree, and validated by the fact that we depend on Pharlux ourselves.

## How it is built

- **Open and inspectable.** The Community edition is free under [AGPL-3.0](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html); the source is there to read.
- **Reproducible and audited.** All dependencies are pinned exact, with `cargo-deny` and `cargo-audit` gates green; the binary is a genuinely static musl build.
- **Production-tested.** V1.0.0 shipped 2026-04-17 after a four-phase delivery plan with hard pass/fail gates: 360 of 360 tests pass, with 10 of 10 consecutive crash-recovery runs and zero flakes.
- **Memory-safe by construction.** Written in Rust, with `rustls` for all TLS and zero OpenSSL in the dependency tree.
