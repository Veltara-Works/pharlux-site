# Pharlux for air-gapped & at-scale deployments

**Applies to: the Pharlux v1.2 line**

Some teams can't send their telemetry to someone else's cloud — not because it's expensive, but because it isn't allowed. Air-gapped networks, data-sovereignty rules, on-prem appliances shipped to customers, environments with no outbound internet at all. For those, a managed SaaS backend isn't a pricier option; it's a non-starter.

Pharlux is a single binary that ingests OpenTelemetry metrics and logs, stores them in local Parquet, and answers SQL queries — all on hardware you control. Nothing egresses. That property is the whole point of this page.

## Runs where the cloud can't

The license — host count and retention ceiling — is carried inside the binary and enforced locally. Pharlux **never phones home**, so it runs fully disconnected. There is no activation server to reach, no usage beacon, no telemetry leaving the box.

- **Zero egress by construction.** Ingest, storage, and query are one process writing to your disk. No third-party data processor sits in the path.
- **Binary-redistribution rights.** The Scale and Custom tiers grant the right to redistribute the Pharlux binary — for shipping it inside an on-prem appliance, or moving it across an air-gap boundary on your own media.
- **Open, portable storage.** Telemetry lands in open Apache Parquet, not a proprietary store you can't read without the vendor. The WAL and per-signal Parquet schemas are frozen (ADR-0018, ADR-0003).

## One node, sized up — on purpose

Pharlux is single-node by design. It scales **up** on one large VPS, not **out** across a cluster — and that is a deliberate constraint, not a missing feature. It's what keeps the operational surface to one binary, one config, and one systemd unit at every tier, from a free Community install to an air-gapped Custom deployment.

One node covers more than the phrase suggests: on a modest **4 vCPU / 8 GB VPS**, Pharlux sustains **350,000 durable metric points per second** with zero errors — a first-party, [reproducible benchmark](/benchmarks), not a headline claim. The ceiling scales with the CPU and disk you give the host.

If you genuinely need multi-region horizontal scale-out with automatic failover, Pharlux is not that system, and we won't pretend otherwise — a managed platform built for planet-scale is the honest choice there. Most teams told they needed that scale never did.

## Flat price, not a meter

The commercial tiers ladder on features, rights, and support — **not** a per-host or per-GB meter. There is no per-host billing, no per-GB ingest charge, no per-million-events indexing fee, and no high-water-mark surprise when something autoscales overnight. Your price is a flat monthly number you can put in a budget twelve months out. Host and retention figures are generous fair-use ceilings, not usage charges.

## You can outlive the vendor

The most common objection at this tier is bus-factor: *what if the vendor disappears?* Source-available software answers it outright.

- **Read and build it yourself.** The Community edition is [AGPL-3.0](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html); the source is there to read, audit, and compile.
- **Your data stays readable.** Frozen WAL and Parquet formats mean a deployment keeps working, and its data keeps being queryable, regardless of what happens to us.
- **Source escrow.** Source escrow is available on request on the Custom tier, so the guarantee can be contractual, not just a licence you take on faith.

Vendor risk is real for any tool. The difference is that only the self-hosted, source-available model hands you the source and the exit.
