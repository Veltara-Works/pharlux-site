# Pharlux vs SigNoz

**Last updated: 2026-07-06** · Applies to: the Pharlux v1.2 line

Pharlux and SigNoz want the same thing: one tool for OpenTelemetry metrics, logs, and traces instead of a stitched-together stack. The difference is what you operate to get there. SigNoz stores everything in **ClickHouse** — a database you run, tune, and back up yourself, joined by ZooKeeper and PostgreSQL once you need high availability. Pharlux embeds its storage: per-signal Parquet on local disk with SQLite for metadata, inside a single 86 MiB binary. Same ambition, far less to operate — which is the whole point for a team running 1–10 services on one VPS.

## At a glance

| | Pharlux v1.2 | SigNoz |
|---|---|---|
| **Signals** | Metrics + logs (traces on the roadmap) | Metrics, logs, traces |
| **OpenTelemetry** | Native — OTLP `:4317` / `:4318` | Native — OTLP-first |
| **Datastore** | Embedded: local Parquet + WAL, SQLite metadata | **ClickHouse** (required), + ZooKeeper + PostgreSQL for HA |
| **What you operate** | 1 binary, 1 config, 1 systemd unit | SigNoz services + ClickHouse (+ ZooKeeper + Postgres at scale) |
| **Query model** | SQL — cross-signal `JOIN` on `trace_id` | Query Builder, ClickHouse SQL, PromQL |
| **Min. self-host footprint** | 2 vCPU / 4 GB baseline | ~4 GB RAM floor (ClickHouse stack) |
| **Throughput (first-party)** | [350k points/sec on 4 vCPU / 8 GB](/benchmarks) | scales with ClickHouse cluster |
| **License** | AGPL-3.0 + commercial | Core MIT (+ Enterprise license) |
| **Self-host cost** | Free (Community) | Free (Community) |

## The ClickHouse question

SigNoz made a reasonable bet: ClickHouse is a genuinely fast columnar database, and leaning on it gives SigNoz strong query performance and cheap storage at scale. The cost of that bet lands on you. ClickHouse is a stateful database with its own operational surface — memory tuning, disk management, backups, and, once you outgrow a single node, sharding via ZooKeeper and a PostgreSQL metadata store. A "single-binary" SigNoz install still has ClickHouse running next to it, with a practical floor around 4 GB of RAM before you ingest anything meaningful.

Pharlux refused that dependency. Storage is a custom write-ahead log plus per-signal Parquet on local disk, with embedded SQLite for metadata — nothing to run beside the binary, nothing to shard, nothing that OOMs at 2 a.m. The trade is deliberate: Pharlux is single-node by design, so it scales up on one box rather than out across a ClickHouse cluster. For its target — a small team on a VPS — that removes an entire category of database operations.

## Why teams pick Pharlux over self-hosting SigNoz

- **One process, not a database to operate.** `sudo pharlux install` and you are ingesting. No ClickHouse to tune, no ZooKeeper, no Postgres, no Compose file of stateful services.
- **SQL across signals in one query.** Cross-signal `JOIN` on `trace_id` is one line. You query metrics and logs through the same Apache DataFusion SQL surface, not a mix of Query Builder and ClickHouse SQL.
- **Runs on hardware you already have.** The Community baseline fits a 2 vCPU / 4 GB VPS; the published benchmark sustains [350,000 metric points/sec on 4 vCPU / 8 GB](/benchmarks), 0 errors over 10.5 million points.
- **A commercial path that isn't "contact sales."** Pharlux lists flat monthly tiers (Community free, Team $49, Business $199, Scale $899) with a tamper-evident audit log included on every paid tier. SigNoz Cloud starts at $49/mo and its Enterprise tier starts around $4,000/mo.
- **Dogfooded in production.** Pharlux is the observability layer for Veltara Works' own ValidonX and Vectis Mail. 459 of 459 tests pass; 10 of 10 consecutive crash-recovery runs with zero flakes.

## Migrating from SigNoz to Pharlux

Because both speak OpenTelemetry, you migrate without a rip-and-replace:

1. Stand up Pharlux on a VPS with `sudo pharlux install` (see the [getting-started guide](https://github.com/Veltara-Works/pharlux/blob/main/docs/user/getting-started.md)).
2. Add a second exporter to your OpenTelemetry Collector pointing at Pharlux on `:4318`, alongside the one feeding SigNoz.
3. Run both in parallel and rebuild the dashboards and alerts you actually use as Pharlux SQL.
4. Retire SigNoz and its ClickHouse once you trust the Pharlux view.

## Is Pharlux the right fit?

SigNoz aims to be a full open-source Datadog: metrics, logs, traces, APM, exceptions, and the scale that ClickHouse unlocks. If you need traces today, or you are ingesting at a volume where a tuned ClickHouse cluster earns its keep, SigNoz is built for that and Pharlux V1 is not — traces are on the roadmap, and Pharlux stays single-node on purpose.

But if you are a small team that wants unified metrics and logs **without** adopting and operating a database to get them, that is exactly the weight Pharlux removes. [Download Pharlux](https://github.com/Veltara-Works/pharlux/releases/latest) and point your Collector at it this afternoon — there is no ClickHouse to stand up first.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the main difference between Pharlux and SigNoz?

Both deliver unified OpenTelemetry observability, but SigNoz stores data in ClickHouse, which you run as a separate database (plus ZooKeeper and PostgreSQL for a clustered setup). Pharlux embeds its storage — per-signal Parquet on local disk with SQLite for metadata — so there is no separate datastore to operate. SigNoz adds traces today; for Pharlux, traces are on the roadmap.

### Does SigNoz require ClickHouse?

Yes. ClickHouse is SigNoz's required datastore for metrics, logs, and traces. A single-node Docker Compose install bundles ClickHouse alongside the SigNoz services; a high-availability deployment adds ZooKeeper for ClickHouse clustering and PostgreSQL for metadata. Pharlux has no external database dependency.

### What is the minimum hardware to self-host SigNoz vs Pharlux?

SigNoz's single-node stack (ClickHouse + collector + the SigNoz binary) has a practical floor around 4 GB of RAM. Pharlux runs comfortably on a 2 vCPU / 4 GB VPS for the Community baseline and is load-tested at 350,000 metric points/sec on 4 vCPU / 8 GB.

### Are Pharlux and SigNoz open source?

Both are open source. SigNoz's core is MIT-licensed with an Enterprise edition under a separate license. Pharlux Community is AGPL-3.0, with a commercial license that removes the AGPL terms and adds host/retention ceilings, a tamper-evident audit log, and support.

### Can I migrate from SigNoz to Pharlux?

Yes, gradually. Both are OpenTelemetry-native, so you add a second exporter to your OpenTelemetry Collector pointing at Pharlux on `:4318` and run both in parallel, then retire SigNoz and its ClickHouse once you trust the Pharlux view.
